## Why Vacuum Sealing Is Essential for Sous Vide

Sous vide literally means "under vacuum" in French. The technique requires food to be sealed in a bag and cooked in temperature-controlled water. Here's why vacuum sealing is the gold standard:

- **No water infiltration** — A proper vacuum seal keeps cooking water out and food juices in
- **Maximum flavor transfer** — Marinades and seasonings stay in direct contact with the food
- **Even cooking** — Tight contact between bag and food ensures consistent heat transfer
- **Professional results** — The same method restaurants use for precise, repeatable cooking

While you *can* use the water displacement method (lowering a zip-top bag into water to push air out), it doesn't compare to a real vacuum seal for reliability and food safety.

Sous vide cooking temperatures range from **120°F to 185°F** (49°C–85°C). Your vacuum bags must be rated for these temperatures without leaching chemicals or losing structural integrity.

## Step-by-Step: Sealing Food for Sous Vide

### 1. Season Your Food

Apply salt, herbs, butter, or marinades before sealing. The vacuum environment intensifies flavor transfer — you'll need less seasoning than you think.

### 2. Handle Liquids Carefully

If you're adding marinade or butter, use the **freeze-first method**:
- Pour your liquid into the bag
- Place the bag upright in the freezer for 30–60 minutes until the liquid solidifies
- Then vacuum seal

This prevents liquid from being pulled into the sealing mechanism. Learn more in our guide to [vacuum sealing liquids](/blog/how-to-vacuum-seal-liquids-without-spilling).

### 3. Position Food Properly

Lay food flat in the bag without overlapping. This ensures even heat distribution during cooking. Leave at least 3 inches of clear space at the bag opening.

### 4. Vacuum Seal

With the FreshLock Pro, place the nozzle over the bag's valve and press the button. The -60kPa suction removes air quickly, creating a tight seal around your food.

### 5. Check the Seal

Before dropping your bag into the water bath, gently squeeze it. If you see air bubbles or feel air pockets, reseal with a fresh bag.

### Long Cooks (24+ Hours)

For extended cooks like brisket or short ribs (up to 72 hours), vacuum sealing is **non-negotiable**. The water displacement method simply won't hold up. Use FreshLock Large bags for these big cuts, and double-check your seal before starting.

## Common Sous Vide Sealing Mistakes to Avoid

### Overfilling the Bag
Leave at least 3 inches at the top. Overfull bags don't seal properly, and food may block the air channel.

### Sealing Sharp Bones
Chicken bones and fish fins can puncture bags during cooking. Trim sharp edges or wrap them in a small piece of paper towel before sealing.

### Not Removing All Air
Trapped air creates insulation, meaning your food cooks unevenly. If you notice air pockets after sealing, start over with a new bag.

### Using Bags Not Rated for Heat
Standard storage bags aren't designed for hot water baths. Always use vacuum bags rated for sous vide temperatures.
