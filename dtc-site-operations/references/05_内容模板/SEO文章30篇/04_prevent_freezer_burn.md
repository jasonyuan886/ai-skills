## What Is Freezer Burn?

Freezer burn happens when **moisture escapes from frozen food** and re-freezes on the surface as ice crystals. It's essentially dehydration — the cold, dry air in your freezer pulls moisture out of your food over time.

The results:
- **Dry, tough texture** — especially noticeable in meats
- **Discoloration** — white or grayish patches on the surface
- **Off flavors** — food tastes bland or "stale"
- **Tougher cooking** — dried-out food doesn't cook evenly

Importantly, freezer burn doesn't make food unsafe to eat — but it does make it unpleasant. And when food tastes bad, it ends up in the trash, which means wasted money.

Now, let's fix that.

## Method 2: Wrap Tight Before Bagging

For extra protection — especially with irregularly shaped items — wrap your food tightly in plastic wrap or parchment paper before vacuum sealing. This creates a moisture barrier and prevents sharp bones from puncturing the vacuum bag.

**Best for:** Whole chickens, bone-in roasts, and anything with sharp edges.

## Method 3: Keep Your Freezer at the Right Temperature

Your freezer should be at **0°F (-18°C)** or below. Here's why temperature matters:

- **Fluctuating temperatures** cause partial thawing and refreezing, which accelerates freezer burn
- **Frost-free freezers** cycle through warming and cooling, which can worsen the problem
- **Overcrowded freezers** restrict airflow, creating uneven temperatures

Keep a freezer thermometer in your freezer and check it monthly. If your freezer runs warm, your food is at risk — no matter how well it's sealed.

## Method 4: Portion Before Freezing

Freezing food in large batches means you'll thaw and refreeze portions — and **every thaw-refreeze cycle degrades quality and invites freezer burn.**

Instead:
1. Divide food into **meal-sized portions** before freezing
2. Vacuum seal each portion individually
3. Only thaw what you'll cook that day

This approach also makes meal planning easier. Grab one bag, cook one meal — no waste, no guesswork.

## Method 5: Label Everything

It sounds simple, but labeling is one of the most effective ways to prevent freezer burn. Here's why:

- **Old food is forgotten food** — forgotten food sits in your freezer for months, accumulating freezer burn
- **Knowing what you have** means you use the oldest items first (FIFO — First In, First Out)
- **Date labels** tell you when something's been in there too long

**What to write on each bag:**
- Food name
- Date sealed
- Portion size or weight
- Any special notes (e.g., "marinated," "pre-cooked")

## Method 6: Avoid Overcrowding Your Freezer

A packed-to-the-brim freezer restricts airflow, creating warm spots where freezer burn thrives. Leave some space between items for cold air to circulate.

## Method 7: Use the Right Bags

Not all freezer bags are created equal. Standard zip-top freezer bags let in small amounts of air over time — enough to cause freezer burn within a few months.

- **BPA-free materials** — safe for food contact at all temperatures
- **Multi-layer construction** — provides superior moisture and oxygen barriers
- **Puncture resistance** — stands up to bones, shells, and rough handling
