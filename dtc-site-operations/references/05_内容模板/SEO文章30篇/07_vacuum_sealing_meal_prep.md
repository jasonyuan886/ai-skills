## Why Vacuum Sealing Is a Meal Prep Secret Weapon

Traditional meal prep containers — even "airtight" ones — still trap air around your food. That air is what causes oxidation, flavor loss, and the dreaded "fridge taste" that makes day-four leftovers so unappealing.

Vacuum sealing removes that air entirely. The result?

- **Food stays fresh longer** — up to 2–3 weeks in the fridge vs. 3–5 days in regular containers
- **Flavors are locked in** — no "fridge taste" absorption
- **Freezer meal prep becomes practical** — vacuum-sealed meals last months, not weeks
- **Portion control is built in** — each bag is a perfectly sized meal
- **Space efficient** — vacuum-sealed bags stack flat, saving tons of fridge and freezer space

## Portioning Strategies

### The Protein + Sides Method
Seal proteins and sides **separately**. Why? Because different foods reheat at different rates. Chicken needs 2 minutes in the microwave; rice needs 1 minute. Sealing them separately means each component reheats perfectly.

### The Complete Meal Method
For convenience, seal an entire meal in one bag. This works best for:
- Soups and stews (pre-frozen flat)
- Casseroles and bakes
- Stir-fry portions (protein + veg together, add sauce when reheating)

### The Freeze-and-Thaw Method
Prep meals for the freezer on Sunday, then move one to the fridge each night to thaw for the next day. Vacuum-sealed meals thaw evenly and taste freshly cooked.

## Sealing Different Food Types

### Proteins (Chicken, Beef, Fish)
1. Cook and let cool completely
2. Portion into meal-sized servings
3. Pat dry to remove excess moisture
4. Seal in appropriately sized bags
5. Label with contents, date, and reheating instructions

### Rice and Grains
1. Cook a large batch
2. Spread on a baking sheet to cool (prevents clumping)
3. Portion into 1-cup or 2-cup servings
4. Seal flat for easy stacking
5. Freeze or refrigerate

### Roasted Vegetables
1. Roast your vegetable medley
2. Cool completely
3. Portion into side-dish servings
4. Seal and freeze
5. Reheat directly from frozen — they'll stay crisp-tender

### Soups and Sauces
Use the **freeze-first method**:
1. Pour soup into a vacuum bag placed inside a container (for support)
2. Freeze until solid
3. Vacuum seal the frozen block
4. Stack flat in the freezer

## Reheating Vacuum-Sealed Meals

### Microwave
1. Cut the bag open (never microwave a sealed bag — it can pop)
2. Transfer to a microwave-safe dish
3. Reheat in 1-minute intervals, stirring between

### Boil-in-Bag
Vacuum-sealed meals can be reheated directly in simmering water:
1. Place the sealed bag in gently boiling water
2. Heat for 5–10 minutes depending on portion size
3. Remove, cut open, and serve

This method is excellent for soups, stews, and rice — it reheats evenly without drying out.

### Sous Vide
For the ultimate reheating method, use sous vide:
1. Drop the sealed bag into a water bath at the food's original cooking temperature
2. Heat until warmed through (30–45 minutes)
3. Your meal tastes like it was just cooked
