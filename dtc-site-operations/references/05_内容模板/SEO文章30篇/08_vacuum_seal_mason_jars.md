Vacuum bags are fantastic for meats, vegetables, and meal prep — but what about dry goods? Coffee beans, rice, nuts, dried herbs, and grains are all pantry staples that lose freshness over time. And while bags work, they're not always the most convenient for items you reach for daily.

Enter the mason jar. When combined with a vacuum sealer, mason jars become airtight storage containers that keep dry goods fresh for months — or even years. No electricity, no spoilage, no waste.

Here's how to do it.

## Why Vacuum Seal Mason Jars?

Mason jars have been a kitchen staple for generations, but traditional canning methods require heat processing and specialized equipment. Vacuum sealing mason jars is faster, easier, and perfect for **dry goods** that don't need heat sterilization.

Benefits include:
- **Extended freshness** — removes oxygen that causes staleness and rancidity
- **Pest protection** — airtight seals keep pantry moths and weevils out
- **Visible storage** — clear glass lets you see exactly what's inside
- **Reusable** — jars and lids can be reused many times (just replace lids when seals weaken)
- **No chemicals** — glass is inert and food-safe
- **Space efficient** — uniform jars stack and organize neatly

### How the Attachment Works

A jar sealer attachment is a simple cap that fits over the rim of a mason jar (with the lid in place). It has a small port that connects to your vacuum sealer's nozzle. When you activate the sealer, it removes air from inside the jar, and atmospheric pressure pushes the lid down — creating an airtight seal.

## Step-by-Step Guide

### Step 1: Prepare Your Jar
1. Wash and thoroughly dry the mason jar and lid
2. Inspect the jar rim for chips or cracks — damaged rims won't seal
3. Use a **fresh lid** for the best seal (previously used lids may have weakened sealing compound)

### Step 2: Fill the Jar
1. Add your dry goods, leaving about **1 inch of headspace** at the top
2. Wipe the rim clean — any debris will prevent a proper seal
3. Place the flat lid on top (do not add the screw band yet)

### Step 3: Attach the Vacuum Sealer
1. Place the jar sealer attachment over the lid, pressing down firmly
2. Connect your FreshLock Pro's nozzle to the attachment port
3. Press the activation button

### Step 4: Seal
1. The vacuum sealer will remove air from the jar (usually takes **10–20 seconds**)
2. You'll hear a change in sound when the vacuum is complete
3. Remove the attachment — the lid should be firmly sealed

### Step 5: Verify the Seal
Press down on the center of the lid. If it doesn't flex or pop, you've got a good seal. If it pops, the seal didn't take — try again with a fresh lid.

### Step 6: Label and Store
Write the contents and date on the lid with a food-safe marker, then store in a cool, dark pantry.

## Foods to Avoid Vacuum Sealing in Jars

Not everything belongs in a vacuum-sealed jar:

- **Moist foods** — vacuum sealing doesn't replace proper canning for wet foods
- **Fresh fruits and vegetables** — they'll spoil without refrigeration
- **Soft cheeses** — moisture can promote bacterial growth in anaerobic conditions
- **Garlic and onions** — risk of botulism in low-oxygen environments (learn more in our guide to [foods you should never vacuum seal](/blog/foods-never-vacuum-seal))

## Storage Tips for Vacuum-Sealed Mason Jars

### Keep Them in a Cool, Dark Place
Light and heat degrade food quality over time. A pantry cupboard or cellar is ideal. Avoid storing jars on sunny windowsills or near heat sources.

### Check Seals Periodically
Every few months, press down on the lids to verify they're still sealed. Temperature fluctuations can occasionally cause seals to release.

### Label Clearly
Use removable labels or write directly on the lid. Include:
- Contents
- Date sealed
- Quantity (helpful for meal planning)

### Rotate Your Stock
Practice FIFO — use the oldest jars first. Most vacuum-sealed dry goods stay fresh for **1–2 years**, but fresher is always better.
